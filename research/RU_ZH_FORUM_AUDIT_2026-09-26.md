# Russian and Chinese Forum Audit — Portal EDL

**Date:** 2026-09-26  
**Question:** Did Russian- or Chinese-language public reports reveal a reproducible EDL/Sahara access path matching this Portal?  
**Scope:** Public-source review only. No firmware-file search and no Sahara packets were sent for this audit.

## Target baseline

The recorded Sahara identity is HWID `0x000620e10137b8a1` (MSM_ID `0x0620e1`, OEM_ID `0x0137`, MODEL_ID `0xb8a1`), mapped by the [bkerler Qualcomm ID table](https://github.com/bkerler/edl/blob/master/edlclient/Config/qualcomm_config.py) to APQ8098. Qualcomm groups APQ8098/MSM8998 on its [product page](https://www.qualcomm.com/processors/application-processors/products/apq8098). In the same public ID table, SDM660 maps to a different MSM_ID (`0x08c0e1`), so Xiaomi/Snapdragon 660 forum reports are not an exact HWID match. These names do not identify the target's exact PBL build or revision. The recorded OEM public-key hash begins `7291ef5c5d99dc05`. The host currently sees `05c6:9008 / QUSB__BULK` at 480 Mbps, session `2996763262814`; this is the same session as the preceding check, not a newly enumerated USB session.

## Findings

### The Chinese Sahara `0x13` write-up is a hypothesis, not a Portal recipe

[Sakura_Rain's CNBlogs article](https://www.cnblogs.com/sakrain/p/-/unlock-your-qualcomm) is the most technically specific Chinese-language result found for Sahara `RESET_STATE_MACHINE` (`0x13`). The author describes an MSM8998/Snapdragon 835 stack-underflow path that reaches an unmapped area and faults with the MMU enabled. The article's list of later chip layouts said to place data or function pointers near the affected stack names SDM665, SDM730, SDM710, SDM845, and SDM855 variants, but omits APQ8098/MSM8998 and SDM660. The author also says the Redmi K20 Pro investigation did not reach a complete reproduction.

The post supplies no Portal PBL revision, APQ8098 stack/frame geometry, certificate-bearing ELF layout, control-flow address, or recoverable payload. Its discussion of signed hash-segment placement and memory initialization describes additional obstacles, not a working bypass. Treat the claimed MSM8998 fault as an unverified secondary hypothesis. It does not explain the Portal's observed timeout by itself and does not define a safe discriminating request.

The separate Snapdragon 8 Elite GBL portion of the same post concerns another boot chain and chipset generation. It is not evidence for this Portal's Sahara EDL path.

### Russian and Chinese Sony Xperable reports are a separate Fastboot route

A Russian [4PDA report for Sony SO-02K / MSM8998](https://4pda.to/forum/index.php?showtopic=847241&st=7900) and a Chinese [Bilibili guide for Sony SO-05K / SDM845](https://www.bilibili.com/opus/1159433473906180098) describe successful Xperable use followed by Sony bootloader unlocking. These reports concern Sony ABL/Fastboot CVE-2021-1931, not Qualcomm EDL/Sahara CVE-2021-30327. The documented steps depend on Sony-specific bootloader builds, XFL data, and matching exploit blobs. The upstream [Xperable README](https://github.com/j4nn/xperable/blob/master/README.rst) limits its support to Sony Yoshino/MSM8998 and Tama/SDM845 devices. Neither forum report supplies a Portal-compatible ABL path or changes the current EDL decision.

Direct access to the 4PDA page was blocked (HTTP 403); the report details came from indexed excerpts. The Bilibili guide was directly opened. These sources establish that a related Qualcomm platform can have a different, vendor-specific Fastboot vulnerability; they do not support transplanting its parameters to the Portal.

### Generic EDL posts explain the signed-programmer boundary

The Chinese [EDL overview](https://www.hztdst.com/5382.html) explains the ordinary PBL/Sahara flow: Sahara authenticates and loads an OEM-signed programmer, and storage operations are provided by that programmer. Its examples use unrelated OnePlus and Xiaomi devices with available vendor loaders. A Russian [4PDA recovery-tool discussion](https://4pda.to/forum/index.php?showtopic=1049146&st=140) similarly describes chip information being available before programmer load and storage access depending on a compatible signed programmer. These are generic recovery explanations, not exploit reports.

### “No-authorization 9008” tool reports describe vendor-specific service paths

The Chinese [Uotan repair thread](https://www.uotan.cn/threads/9008xian-shua-jiu-zhuan-ji-fang-ge-ji-ji-chu-zhi-shi-ke-pu.38183/) says the author's Qualcomm toolbox supports no-authorization flashing on some devices. The same explanation says that after a programmer is loaded, some vendors (including Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, and Meizu) add a separate authorization step before storage read/write. A [Bilibili guide](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1dwheetEZi/) advertises Xiaomi/Redmi/Black Shark examples on Snapdragon 636, 660, 680, 845, 855, 865, and 870. It does not name Snapdragon 835/MSM8998 or APQ8098. These examples are model/OEM-specific recovery or post-programmer authorization paths; the public posts do not link them to a Portal-compatible Sahara exploit.

The [public MEDL845 repository](https://github.com/ColdWindScholar/MEDL845/blob/master/%E5%B0%8F%E7%B1%B3845%E5%85%8D%E5%8F%97%E6%9D%839008%E5%B7%A5%E5%85%B7.py) is a Xiaomi Snapdragon 845 utility wrapper. Its Python code delegates flashing to a bundled `MIQC845Flash.exe` and has Xiaomi model/MIUI-specific unlock functions; the underlying flashing implementation is not present in the repository. That makes it evidence of a device-scoped service tool, not a reviewable APQ8098 PBL exploit. The 4PDA [Xiaomi EDL-auth discussion](https://4pda.to/forum/index.php?showtopic=643084&st=7580) lists MSM8998 Mi 6/Mi Mix 2 among the devices affected by Xiaomi's account authorization change. That is Xiaomi's OEM service authorization layer, separate from the PBL's signed-programmer gate.

Oxygen Forensics is also mentioned in the CNBlogs discussion, but its public [EDL extraction methods sheet](https://oxygenforensics.com/uploads/press_kit/Device_Extraction_Methods.pdf) lists Huawei Qualcomm EDL support for MSM8917, MSM8937, MSM8940, and SDM450; it does not list APQ8098/MSM8998. Direct PDF access returned 403, so this check relies on the indexed excerpt. This does not prove the vendor has no private or later support, but no public support statement matching this Portal surfaced. A 4PDA user also reported Oxygen and Magnet failing on a separate EDL case; that anecdote is not a Portal test.

An additional Chinese-language community overview says public evidence has made Snapdragon 835/MSM8998 PBL exploitation difficult, while citing 845/855 tools as practical examples ([community article](https://mlmistrevolutionagain.codeberg.page/revpost/13/D.%E6%8A%80%E6%9C%AF%E4%B8%8E%E5%AE%89%E5%85%A8%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6/3.%E5%88%B7%E6%9C%BA%E4%B8%8E%E5%AE%89%E5%85%A8.html)). It is a broad secondary summary without citations to a Portal or APQ8098 reproduction, so it supports only the same caution already expressed by the CNBlogs author.

### Russian forum evidence confirms the target-specific data gap

In the long [4PDA Qualcomm recovery thread](https://4pda.to/forum/index.php?showtopic=643084&st=9660), a 2022 participant asked for PBL dumps from Qualcomm platforms at or newer than MSM8998/Snapdragon 835. Replies questioned the practical value and did not provide a dump. This indexed exchange is weak evidence about community availability, not proof that no dump exists. The same thread has a first-hand OnePlus 5T/MSM8998 post describing a device stuck in Sahara Download even after a vendor flashing attempt ([4PDA posts around #8433](https://4pda.to/forum/index.php?showtopic=643084&st=8420)); it contains no Sahara bypass or successful recovery path.

The same long thread has a useful mode-identification anecdote in [posts #3922–3924](https://4pda.to/forum/index.php?showtopic=643084&st=3920). The Windows USB name remains `QDLoader 9008` in both cases, but a participant's `emmcdl -info` output showed `SBL SW Version: 0x00000004`; a contributor interpreted that as a limited SBL pseudo-download mode and said a zero version was expected for PBL. This is forum guidance, not a protocol specification, but it explains why `05c6:9008` alone cannot prove execution in PBL. The Portal's earlier read-only Sahara `GET_SBL_VERSION` query (`0x07`) returned four zero bytes in session `2300689565969`, which is consistent with PBL under this heuristic. It still does not fingerprint the PBL revision or prove exploitability.

### No Portal-matched Russian or Chinese report surfaced

Searches covered CNBlogs, CSDN, Zhihu, Tieba/52pojie results, Bilibili, V2EX, and 4PDA using combinations of `CVE-2021-30327`, `RESET_STATE_MACHINE`, `MSM8998`, `APQ8098`, `Aloha`, `9008`, `Sahara`, and `EDL`. Results included generic repair guidance, translated or reposted vulnerability summaries, and the Sony-specific Fastboot examples above. No source found reported a first-hand Portal test, a matching APQ8098 PBL revision, or reproducible Portal-specific exploit parameters.

The full [Qualcomm CVE-2021-30327 record](https://github.com/CVEProject/cvelistV5/blob/main/cves/2021/30xxx/CVE-2021-30327.json) is relevant to the chipset family, but the family-level affected entry does not provide this Portal's PBL revision or an exploit profile. The official [Hexacon 2023 talk page](https://2023.hexacon.fr/conference/speakers/) describes the Sahara recursion research at a high level; it likewise does not supply Portal-matched parameters.

## Decision

The non-English forum pass adds context but no new Portal-specific EDL test. The “no-auth” examples found are tied to Xiaomi/Oppo device profiles or an OEM post-programmer authorization stage; public material does not show that they bypass this Portal's OEM signature gate or accept its HWID. Existing notes record that the Portal's bounded `0x13` reset/hash check did not change the OEM-key hash before its timeout boundary; repeated reset loops and guessed ELF/address variants are already closed. Keep the device connected in EDL while seeking a documented APQ8098/Portal-matched trigger and PBL profile. Resume live protocol testing only when a source provides a bounded, discriminating request with parameters tied to this target. Do not treat the Sony Fastboot route, the Xiaomi service tools, or the CNBlogs hypothesis as such a profile.

### Static mapping of the SM8450 report's `RECV_DATA` label

The SM8450 author's [Medium report](https://medium.com/@shurikgo/experimental-confirmation-of-cve-2026-25262-on-snapdragon-8-gen-1-sm8450-4ee86504b768) says the modified client was based on B.Kerler's `edl` and `upload_loader`, logs Sahara protocol version 3, and describes setting `data_offset` before sending data. B.Kerler's published [`sahara_defs.py`](https://github.com/bkerler/edl/blob/master/edlclient/Library/sahara_defs.py) defines the standard transfer request names `SAHARA_READ_DATA` (`0x03`) and `SAHARA_64BIT_MEMORY_READ_DATA` (`0x12`), but no wire constant named `SAHARA_CMD_RECV_DATA`; its [`upload_loader`](https://github.com/bkerler/edl/blob/master/edlclient/Library/sahara.py) handles those target requests by selecting the requested file slice and sending raw bytes. Qualcomm's [qdl source](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/external/qdl/+/refs/heads/android16-qpr1-release/sahara.c) and [Sahara protocol definitions in the Qualcomm-authored Linux driver](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/accel/qaic/sahara.c) separately list `READ_DATA` `0x03` at protocol 1.0, `READ_DATA64` `0x12` at 2.8, and `WRITE_DATA` `0x14` at 3.0. The qdl implementation handles the two READ_DATA request IDs by selecting a file slice and sending the bytes; it does not handle `WRITE_DATA` in its packet dispatcher.

This narrows, but does not close, the version question. The SM8450 report does not publish `cve_final_single`, a packet capture, or the patched method body, so its `RECV_DATA` label cannot be assigned confidently to wire command `0x14` versus a modified `READ_DATA`/`READ_DATA64` transfer path. If it means `WRITE_DATA` `0x14`, the report's v3 method does not fit the Portal's v2 HELLO. If it means an ordinary READ_DATA request/response, that command family predates v3, but the report does not establish that the vulnerable memory-write behavior is shared by APQ8098. Do not send `0x14` or manufacture a v2 data-transfer packet from the prose; resolve the exact packet and target-side semantics first. No live command follows from this source review.

### CVE-2021-30327 and the older no-loader QFPROM claim

The Qualcomm-assigned [CVE-2021-30327 record](https://github.com/CVEProject/cvelistV5/blob/main/cves/2021/30xxx/CVE-2021-30327.json) explicitly lists APQ8098/MSM8998 and describes a Sahara command-processing buffer overflow that can overwrite secure-configuration data. This is a genuine SoC-family lead for the Portal, but the record gives no triggering command, PBL revision range, or packet recipe. The public Katana implementation inspected locally has only an SDM845 profile; the Chinese CNBlogs author says MSM8998 stack underflow reaches unmapped memory and their own exploit was incomplete. These sources do not combine into a Portal-ready PoC.

A separate 2019 NoCon slide deck by Bjoern Kerler claims a no-loader --qfp check for devices with disabled/improper QFPROM secure-boot fuses. It lists 0x00780350 for MSM8998, says zero means secure boot is disabled, and gives cc3153a80293939b as a common weak-hash prefix; it also says SDM660/SDM845 devices should be fine. The slide's embedded Link destination is not preserved in the accessible copy, and no reproducible source or command was found. The Portal's observed OEM-key hash begins 7291ef5c5d99dc05, not the cited prefix. B.Kerler's current edl qfp implementation requires a Firehose loader with peek support (documented for EL3 loaders), so it is not the same no-loader test. Treat the old claim as an unverified, fuse-conditional lead; it does not identify a safe command for this Portal.
